Who’s who

The Players that Protect You.

Six concepts worth understanding before investing in Dubai. We explain it Crystal clear.

01

Dubai Land Department (DLD)

The government body that registers every property, sale and lease in Dubai. Think of it as the land registry and the cadastre in one.

02

RERA

The DLD’s regulatory agency. It licenses and supervises developers, projects, agencies and agents, approves listings and monitors escrow accounts.

03

Escrow account

The dedicated bank account for each off-plan project (Law 8/2007). Your money stays there and is only released to the developer as construction progresses.

04

Oqood

The provisional registration of your off-plan purchase with the DLD. It proves the unit is yours while it is being built, and becomes the title deed at handover.

05

SPA (Sale and Purchase Agreement)

The contract with the developer. It sets the price, the payment plan, the expected handover date and what happens if either side defaults.

06

Title deed

The final ownership deed the DLD issues in your name once the property is handed over and fully paid.

What if something goes wrong?

What the law says
if there are problems

We believe in being clear from the start. Here’s how we protect your project if challenges arise.

Handover delay

If construction is delayed

Your SPA sets the expected date and the grace period. If it is exceeded, you can claim under the terms of the contract. Money not yet justified by construction stays in the escrow account.

Project cancelled

If RERA cancels the project

The developer must refund what buyers have paid, and the refund is handled through the project’s escrow account under DLD supervision.

Disputes

Where to file a claim

Dubai has a special judicial committee for unfinished or cancelled projects. Its decisions are binding and are enforced through the Dubai courts.

Sources: Law No. 8 of 2007 on escrow accounts; Law No. 13 of 2008 on the interim real estate register and its amendments; Dubai Land Department. General information, not legal advice: each case depends on the signed contract.

Payment methods

Pay the way that suits you

Payments goes direct to the Developer.

Dubai is one of the few markets in the world that accepts so many ways to pay for a home, always with the traceability the law requires.

Bank transfer

An international transfer from your bank, in dollars, euros or pesos, straight to the project’s escrow account.

Credit card

Many developers accept cards for the reservation and for the payment-plan instalments.

Cryptocurrency

More and more developers accept crypto, through regulated platforms that convert it into dirhams.

Cash

Cash is also accepted at the developer’s offices, subject to identity and source-of-funds checks.

Accepted payment methods depend on each developer or seller. Under anti-money-laundering rules, cash payments from about $15,000 USD (AED 55,000) and all crypto payments are identified and reported to the authorities. We’ll tell you which options each project accepts.

Check it yourself

Don’t take our word for it: to check it yourself.

Everything important can be checked on the Dubai Land Department’s official website or in its free Dubai REST app. That includes everything we tell you.

1

Is the project registered?

With the DLD’s Project Status service you can look up the project and see whether it is registered, who the developer is and what stage it is at.

Check the project status →
2

How is construction going?

The DLD’s Project Status service shows the completion percentage of every project. Compare it with the construction photos and videos we send you.

See construction progress →
3

Is it the right escrow account?

The account you pay into must appear in your SPA and be in the project’s name. Check it in the DLD’s Project Status service: find the project and open the Escrow tab, where the official escrow account is shown. Never pay into personal accounts.

See the project’s escrow account →
4

Is your agent licensed?

Every agent holds a professional card with a BRN number, and every agency an ORN number. Search for your agent by name in the DLD’s official register of licensed brokers.

Find your agent in the DLD register →
5

Is the price right?

Every sale is registered with the DLD. In the DLD’s open data you can see what similar units in the same building or area have sold for.

Search real transactions →
6

Is the unit in my name?

With your Oqood certificate or your title deed you can check with the DLD that the property is registered in your name.
